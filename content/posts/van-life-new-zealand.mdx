We picked up the van in Christchurch on a Tuesday — a white Toyota HiAce with a bed in the back, a small sink, and a cassette toilet we agreed never to mention. The rental guy showed us how to fold the seats into a mattress, handed over the keys, and said, "Don't drive at night. The roads aren't built for it." We did not always listen. Here is what two weeks on the South Island taught us.

## The Route

Fourteen days is barely enough. We went counterclockwise — south first, then up the West Coast — and still skipped entire regions. Here is the spine of the trip:

```text
# South Island Van Route (14 days)

Christchurch → Lake Tekapo          Day 1
Tekapo → Mount Cook → Wanaka        Day 2-3
Wanaka → Queenstown                 Day 4-5
Queenstown → Te Anau → Milford      Day 6-7
Milford → Queenstown → Fox Glacier  Day 8
Fox Glacier → Hokitika              Day 9
Hokitika → Abel Tasman              Day 10-11
Abel Tasman → Kaikoura             Day 12
Kaikoura → Christchurch             Day 13-14
```

## The Highlights

### Milford Sound

The drive from Te Anau to Milford is 119 kilometers and takes three hours, because you will stop every ten minutes. Homer Tunnel drops you into a rainforest valley walled by cliffs. The sound itself — actually a fjord — is waterfalls and black rock and dolphins if you are lucky. Take the early boat. Stay dry. You won't.

### Mount Cook at Dawn

We parked at the Hooker Valley trackhead and slept in the van. At 5:30 a.m. the sky went pink over Aoraki and we walked the valley in silence. No one was there. This is the trick of van life: you sleep where others day-trip, and you see the places at the hours they cannot.

### Wanaka's Lone Tree

Yes, it is in every influencer's feed. Yes, the lake at sunrise is still worth it. Walk past the crowds to the north shore — the tree looks better from behind anyway.

## Where You Sleep

### Holiday Parks (Powered Sites)

Most nights we paid NZ$20-30 per person for a powered site at a holiday park — hot showers, a kitchen, sometimes a hot tub. Top 10 Holiday Parks are reliable; DOC (Department of Conservation) campsites are cheaper (NZ$15) and more beautiful, but basic.

### Freedom Camping

Freedom camping is legal only in self-contained vehicles (certified). Our rental was. Even so, the spots fill by sunset, and rangers fine you for parking outside designated zones. Use the Rankers Camping NZ app to find legal sites.

## What Van Life Actually Feels Like

It is not the Instagram version. The sink is too small. The bed is never quite flat. You argue about who forgot to fill the water jug. You wake at 4 a.m. because the sun comes up early and the birds are loud.

But you also wake up next to a glacial lake. You make coffee with the doors open to a fjord. You fall asleep listening to a river. The van is small, and that is the point — it puts you outside, in the landscape, all the time.

New Zealand's South Island is the most beautiful place I have driven. Two weeks was not enough. It will not be enough next time either. We will go back.
