Iceland's Route 1 — the Ring Road — loops 1,332 kilometers around the entire country, threading waterfalls, glaciers, volcanic plains, and fishing villages into a single drivable loop. In seven days you can circle the island and still have time to stand, shivering and awestruck, at the edge of something vast.

## Why Seven Days Is the Sweet Spot

You can rush the Ring Road in five days, but you'll spend half your time in the car. Ten days is dreamy but not everyone can spare it. Seven days hits the balance: enough stops to feel each region, enough driving to keep momentum, and one buffer morning for weather (because in Iceland, there is always weather).

### Renting a Car

Book a 4x4 — not for the Ring Road itself, which is paved almost entirely, but for the F-roads (highland mountain tracks) you'll be tempted to explore. Roads like F208 to Landmannalaugar require four-wheel drive and are legally off-limits without it. Insurance matters here: take the sand-and-ash coverage and the gravel protection. The wind alone can repaint your car with volcanic grit.

## The Itinerary, Day by Day

### Day 1: Reykjavík to Vík

Leave Reykjavík early and head east on Route 1. Your first stop is Seljalandsfoss, the waterfall you can walk behind — bring a raincoat. A short path north leads to the hidden Gljúfrabúi, which pours from a slot canyon you wade into. Next is Skógafoss, a 60-meter wall of water with a staircase to its summit.

End the day in Vík, a village of 300 people beneath looming sea stacks. Eat lamb soup at Suður Vík and sleep early.

### Day 2: Vík to Jökulsárlón

Drive through the lava fields of Eldhraun, then stop at Fjaðrárgljúfur, a serpentine green canyon. The day's climax is Jökulsárlón, the glacier lagoon where icebergs the size of houses drift toward the sea. Walk across the road to Diamond Beach, where those same icebergs wash up on black sand like scattered glass.

### Day 3: East Fjords to Egilsstaðir

The East Fjords are the quietest stretch of the Ring Road — sheer mountains dropping into the sea, tiny fishing villages, and reindeer that wander down to the road. Pull over often. Sleep in Egilsstaðir, the hub of the east.

### Day 4: Detour to Mývatn

Today you leave the Ring Road briefly to hit the geothermal north. The Mývatn Nature Baths are the locals' Blue Lagoon — same milky-blue water, a fraction of the crowds. Nearby, the boiling mud pots of Hverir smell of sulfur and look like another planet.

### Day 5: Goðafoss and Akureyri

Stop at Goðafoss, the "Waterfall of the Gods," then continue to Akureyri, Iceland's second city (population 19,000). Its church, Akureyrarkirkja, mirrors Reykjavík's Hallgrímskirkja. Grab a beer at the local microbrewery.

### Day 6: The North Coast to Húnaflói

Drive the long, lonely northern shore. Watch for horses — the Icelandic breed is small, shaggy, and uniquely five-gaited. Many farms offer a quick tölt ride.

### Day 7: Back to Reykjavík

Close the loop through Borgarfjörður. If time allows, detour to Hraunfossar, a waterfall that seeps directly from a lava field into a turquoise river. Return the car in Reykjavík and reward yourself with a hot dog from Bæjarins Beztu.

Groceries are your friend. Bonus and Krónan supermarkets stock everything you need for sandwich lunches and pasta dinners — eating every meal at restaurants will double your food costs.

## Practical Tips

- **Fuel up whenever you can.** Gas stations can be 100 km apart in the east and north.
- **Check vedur.is daily.** The Icelandic Met Office is gospel. Storms can close roads in any season.
- **Drive to the conditions.** A 90 km/h limit exists, but wind and ice dictate the real speed.
- **Never stop on the road for photos.** Icelanders have a word for tourists who do. Use the pullouts.

> The land here is still being made. Drive it slowly, and you are driving through creation itself.

Seven days is enough to fall in love with Iceland. It is not enough to feel you have finished it — but that is the point. The Ring Road is a loop, and loops, by nature, invite return.
