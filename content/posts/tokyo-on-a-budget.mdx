Tokyo has a reputation for being expensive, and parts of it are — a single sushi omakase can cost more than a flight. But the Tokyo most travelers fall in love with is the cheap one: the standing soba counter, the park at dawn, the ¥100 cans of coffee from vending machines. Here is how to spend five days in the city for under $500, excluding your flight.

## The Budget Breakdown

Let's be honest about the math before we start. Here is roughly what five days costs if you stay disciplined:

```text
# Tokyo 5-Day Budget (USD)
# Excludes international flights and travel insurance.

Accommodation (capsule hotel x5 nights):  $150
Food (konbini + cheap eats):              $120
Transport (Suica + 24h passes):            $45
Attractions (mostly free):                 $30
Misc (coffee, snacks, sim card):           $55
Onsen entry (1x):                          $15
Buffer:                                    $85
                                           ─────
Total:                                    $500
```

## Where You Sleep

### Capsule Hotels

Skip the $200 business hotels. Capsule hotels in Asakusa or Shinjuku run $25-35 a night and are cleaner, quieter, and more Japanese than any Hilton. You get a bed in a pod, shared baths, and a locker. Bring earplugs.

### Hostels With a Twist

If capsules feel claustrophobic, try a hostel like K's House or UNPLAN — about $30 a night, with kitchens where you can cook rice and grill cheap supermarket fish.

## Where You Eat

This is where Tokyo rewards the broke traveler. Cheap food here is not compromise food; it is some of the best in the world.

- **Konbini (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson):** Onigiri for ¥120, egg sandwiches for ¥180, fried chicken after 9 p.m. at a discount. A full breakfast for $3.
- **Standing soba (tachigui):** Bowl of soba for ¥300-500, eaten at a counter in under five minutes. Look for the stalls near train stations.
- **Ramen chains:** Ichiran, Ippudo, local shops in Shinjuku's Omoide Yokocho. A bowl is ¥800-1,000 and fills you for hours.
- **Supermarket bento:** After 7 p.m., department store basements discount sushi and bento by 30-50%.

## What You Do for Free

### Parks and Shrines

Meiji Shrine is free. Yoyogi Park is free. The Imperial Palace East Gardens are free (closed Mondays). Senso-ji in Asakusa is free and overwhelming at dawn.

### Free Viewpoints

Skip Tokyo Tower's paid deck. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building in Shinjuku has free observation decks on the 45th floor — go at sunset, when Mount Fuji sometimes appears on the horizon.

## Getting Around

Buy a Suica or Pasmo IC card at any station. Load ¥1,000 at a time. Within Tokyo, most trips cost ¥170-260. A 24-hour Tokyo Metro pass is ¥600 — worth it only if you take four or more rides in a day.

## The One Splurge

Book one onsen. A sento in Shimokitazawa or a larger bathhouse in Odaiba will run you ¥500-2,000. Soaking in hot mineral water after walking 20,000 steps is the most Tokyo feeling there is.

## The Real Cost

Tokyo on $500 is not a trick. It is the default way locals live. The expensive version — Michelin kaiseki, neon-lit sky bars — is a thin layer over a city built for people who eat cheap, walk far, and pay attention. Bring good shoes. Bring an empty stomach. The rest takes care of itself.
